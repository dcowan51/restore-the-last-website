  1. 1 / 8

    La Casa de mi Padre: My Father's House.

    A Christ-centered children's home in El Salvador. Courts place children there after abuse, neglect or abandonment.

  2. 2 / 8

    Everyone gets a session. Kids and grown-ups.

    Children, house parents, teachers and staff. When the adults around them are calm, the children have a real chance.

  3. 3 / 8

    More than 18,000 sessions, and counting.

    Delivered on-site, day after day, by a trained practitioner who also serves the children at a partner school.

  4. 4 / 8

    26 of 28 people got better.

    In a 2022 study there, 26 of the 28 people scored improved. On average, symptoms fell by about half after one session.

  5. 5 / 8

    Thrive: a home for kids who aged out.

    A 25-acre campus in Arizona for young adults who left foster care. Many arrived with no family to call.

  6. 6 / 8

    “The results have paid for itself.”

    After three years of sessions on campus, Thrive's CEO Steve Vogel says the results have paid for themselves, “just exponentially.”

  7. 7 / 8

    Rite Path: two boxes, used every day.

    In Phoenix, Rite Path's staff have used two CalmBoxes for two years, several times a day. Now they're asking for ten.

  8. 8 / 8

    Three homes so far. The next one is waiting.

    Each began with someone who believed it could help. The next home is waiting on the same thing.

What the El Salvador study can't tell you+

No control group: everyone received sessions, so we can't separate their effect from time, attention or hope.

Self-reported: symptoms were rated by the participants, some of them children, with hopeful adults around them.

Uneven spacing: sessions fit around a working school, not a research design. What would settle it is a controlled study with fixed intervals and follow-up. We haven't run one yet.

The 26 of 28 counts the people who could be scored (29 took part; one had no symptoms to start with).
